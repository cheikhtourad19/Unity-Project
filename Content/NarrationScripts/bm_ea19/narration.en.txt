Here is the upper part of a colossal statue of Ramesses the Second. It comes from the Ramesseum, his mortuary temple at Thebes. The king wears the nemes, the striped headdress of the pharaohs. Look at the face, the shoulders and the variations in the stone’s colour. Even as a fragment, this sculpture retains a powerful presence. It shows how monuments could make royal authority visible and connect the ruler’s image with sacred spaces.
